**WHAT IT IS**

Learning AI is not just about coursework. It is about encountering the people doing the work, understanding how they think, what problems they are wrestling with, and what they see coming next. That is what this series is for.

Inspirational

Real journeys from African and global AI practitioners across research, industry, policy, and entrepreneurship, so you can see viable paths for yourself.

Technical and deep

Expert-level knowledge on frontier AI that goes further than standard cohort curriculum. Active research directions, cutting-edge tools, applied methods.

Africa-grounded

Open research questions and innovation opportunities relevant to African contexts — informing the research cohort and sparking real project ideas.

Ecosystem-connected

Each session connects the TRI AI community to academic labs, industry teams, policy bodies, and peers.

Format

One Saturday a month. Two hours. Open to everyone.

Sessions are virtual-first and livestreamed, with written summaries shared the following week via the TRI AI newsletter.

When: Last Sunday of every month, 4:00 PM – 6:00 PM WAT

Structure: 60-minute talk · 30-minute Q&A · 30-minute community discussion

Platform: Zoom (live) and YouTube (livestream and recording)

Speakers: African researchers, diaspora practitioners, global academics, and industry builders

After each session: Written summary notes sent via the TRI AI newsletter within one week

Cost:Free, always

Themes

Five tracks. Rotating through the year.

Sessions rotate across these themes to maintain variety while building cumulative depth over time. The rotation is flexible, a major model release or African AI policy development can shift the schedule.

Theme A: Research frontiers

State-of-the-art ML and AI research: large language models, diffusion models, RLHF, multi-modal AI, graph neural networks, and in-context learning. Straight from research labs and academia.

Theme B: AI for Africa

African NLP, speech and accent technology, African image datasets, AI for healthcare and agriculture, climate AI, local language models, and building datasets for underrepresented languages. The most distinctive theme in the series.

Theme C: AI in industry and product

Building and deploying AI products: MLOps, model evaluation, responsible AI in production, AI startup journeys, and career paths in AI engineering. Grounded in practice.

Theme D: Ethics, policy, and governance

Bias and fairness, AI policy in Africa, data sovereignty, algorithmic accountability, AI safety, and privacy. Not optional, rather, essential to building AI that actually serves communities.

Theme E: Inspiration and pathways

Career journeys, community building, research culture, mentorship, and the experience of navigating the AI field as an African professional. Deliberately personal.
